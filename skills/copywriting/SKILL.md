# Copywriting

## The Specificity Test

Read every line and ask: **could a competitor paste this verbatim?** If yes, it is filler. Rewrite with a noun only this product owns — a role, a file format, a number, a named workflow.

## Headline Patterns

1. **Claim + mechanism** — "Cuts deploy time to 90 seconds by caching the dependency graph"
2. **Tension + resolution** — "Your schema drifted three weeks ago. Vault found it in one."
3. **Concrete noun + verb** — "Vault reads your Terraform state and writes the diff"

Length: 6-12 words. Break at a meaning boundary, never mid-phrase.

## 8 Rewrites

- BEFORE "Everything your team needs to move quickly" AFTER "Ships the migration your staff engineer keeps postponing"
- BEFORE "Powerful analytics at your fingertips" AFTER "Every query, with the exact row count it scanned"
- BEFORE "Built for modern teams" AFTER "Built for the 4-person team on-call for 40 services"
- BEFORE "Seamless integrations" AFTER "Reads Postgres, Snowflake and one CSV you drag in"
- BEFORE "Trusted by industry leaders" AFTER "Runs payroll for 1,180 clinics in 14 states"
- BEFORE "Lightning-fast performance" AFTER "p95 response 41ms, measured at 12k req/s"
- BEFORE "Take your workflow to the next level" AFTER "Close the month in two days, not nine"
- BEFORE "Our mission is to democratize data" AFTER "A finance analyst should not need to file a ticket to see yesterday"

## Craft Per Slot

- **Eyebrow** (10-24 chars): category or status, not a sentence — "Schema drift", "Beta - invite only"
- **Subhead** (12-28 words): says *how*, never repeats the headline's nouns. It earns its place by adding the mechanism the headline claimed.
- **CTA**: verb + object the user recognizes — "Scan my repo", "See a live migration", "Read the 4-min setup". One primary; the secondary is a text link.
- **Proof point**: number + unit + source + date — "63% fewer rollbacks (n=214 deploys, Q2 2025)". A number with no source reads as invented; either source it or cut it.

## Coining a Name

Two syllables or one compound, 4-8 letters, pronounceable on first read, not a dictionary noun: Vault, Ledger, Kestrel, Northpin, Casva, Tallow. Then use it as a subject with real verbs — "Kestrel watches the branch", not "the platform enables". Never switch between the name and "our platform" mid-page.

## Voice Calibration

- **Technical** — "Runs as a single binary. No agent, no sidecar, no privileged container."
- **Editorial** — "The outage was never the deploy. It was the config nobody owned."
- **Warm** — "You will see the first diff in about a minute. We will email you if it takes longer."
- **Severe** — "Unreviewed access expires in 24 hours. There is no extension."

Pick one per page and hold it. Mixed voice reads as a committee.

## DECIDE

- Which noun on this page is unique to the product? If none, stop and get a fact.
- Is the subhead adding mechanism, or restating the headline?
- Does every number carry a source and a date?
- Is the CTA a verb the user already says out loud?
- Read the hero aloud. Does it sound like a person who has used the thing?

## NEVER

"Everything you need", "Powerful yet simple", "Coming soon", "Lorem ipsum", "Get started today", "Take your X to the next level", "Seamlessly", "Unlock", "Supercharge", emoji bullets, three-adjective stacks ("fast, simple, secure"), ALL-CAPS shouting in body copy, a testimonial with no name and role.
